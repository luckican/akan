The Office of the Special Prosecutor has opened a preliminary investigation into a disputed memorandum that alleges a GH¢70,000 payment was connected to Parliament's recent vetting of ministerial and Supreme Court nominees.

The investigation is the verified development. The payment allegation remains unproven.

In a notice on 4 September 2026, the OSP said the circulating document is presented as a memo from Deputy Minority Leader Patricia Appiagyei to Minority Chief Whip Frank Annoh-Dompreh. According to the OSP, the document alleges that Appiagyei returned GH¢70,000 described as an advance from the Majority Chief Whip in relation to the Appointments Committee's vetting of two ministerial nominees and three Supreme Court nominees.

The OSP says it is trying to establish three things: whether the document is genuine, where the alleged funds came from and what they were for. It has invited Appiagyei to assist with the preliminary investigation. The office said the constitutional importance of the Appointments Committee and the need to safeguard the integrity of the vetting process justified opening the inquiry.

Appiagyei denies the premise. She told Graphic Online that the memo was fake and later issued a statement saying she did not write, authorise or circulate it. She also said she did not receive GH¢70,000, or any other payment, in connection with the vetting and therefore returned no money.

We have not treated the alleged payment as fact because the OSP itself is still testing the memo's authenticity, source and purpose. The inquiry may establish whether the document is genuine and whether any money changed hands. Until then, the official record supports an investigation, not a finding of wrongdoing.
